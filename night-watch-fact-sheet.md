# Rembrandt's *The Night Watch* (De Nachtwacht) — Fact Sheet

## 1) Official Rijksmuseum Record (object number SK-C-5)

- **Title:** Het korporaalschap van kapitein Frans Banninck Cocq en luitenant Willem van Ruytenburch, bekend als de 'Nachtwacht'
- **Maker:** Rembrandt van Rijn (Rembrandt)
- **Dating:** 1642
- **Materials and technique:** Oil on canvas (physical characteristics: oil on canvas; dimensions recorded as height 379.5 cm × width 453.5 cm; weights 337 kg and 170 kg)
- **Exact dimensions as given:** height 379.5 cm × width 453.5 cm

## 3) The 18th-Century Story (verified against Rijksmuseum record, English Wikipedia, and Rijksmuseum site)

- Painted: Rembrandt painted the militia company portrait in 1642 at the peak of the Dutch Golden Age.
- Commission: commissioned around 1639 by Captain Frans Banninck Cocq and 17 of his civic militia guards (schutterij), for the newly built Kloveniersdoelen (Musketeers' Meeting Hall) in Amsterdam. (Sources: Rijksmuseum & English Wikipedia.)
- Moved to Amsterdam's Town Hall: In 1715, the painting was moved to the Amsterdam Town Hall, where it was trimmed on all four sides, presumably to fit it between two columns. This resulted in the loss of two figures on the left side and parts of the arch/balustrade/edge of the step. (Sources: Rijksmuseum & English Wikipedia.)
- Years after completion: 1642 → 1715 = 173 years.
- Notable additions the web sources mention but the record itself doesn't:
  - The carrying company emblem of the arquebusiers retained in the figure of the girl with the dead chicken; the firming of the shields and names (18 shields, Dutch: "hollands" blijck; the names of 34 characters are inscribed).
  - It is thought the painting was completed in a lean-to in Rembrandt's garden because it was too large for the Amsterdam studio.
  - Napoleon's occupation after the fire of the Netherlands: after Napoleon occupied the Netherlands, the Town Hall became the Palace on the Dam and the magistrates moved the painting to the Trippenhuis; Napoleon ordered it returned, but after the occupation ended in 1813 the painting again moved to the Trippenhuis (now the Dutch Academy of Sciences), until it moved to the new Rijksmuseum when the building was finished in 1885.
  - It was removed from the Rijksmuseum in September 1939 at the onset of World War II, rolled around a cylinder, stored four years in Maastricht, and re-mounted, restored, and returned after the war.
  - Vandalism attacks: 1911 (shoemaker with knife, varnish protected), 1975 (bread knife attack by Wilhelmus de Rijk, restoring after four years), 1990 (acid spray on an escaped psychiatric patient), and 2019 onwards Operation Night Watch restoration.
